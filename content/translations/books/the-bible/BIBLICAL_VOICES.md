# biblical voices: character voice mapping

## purpose

this document establishes consistent character voices for the brainrot bible translation. each major character gets 3-5 signature brainrot terms that define their speech patterns and narrator descriptions. maintaining these voices across all 66 books ensures recognizable, memorable characterization while hitting maximum brainrot density.

**critical rule**: once a character's voice is established here, it must remain consistent across all appearances in every book. update character tracking files as you go.

## verse-level fidelity (non-negotiable)

**the atomic unit of biblical translation is the verse.**

- each translated chapter MUST have the exact same number of verses as the source
- each verse MUST be numbered with its reference (e.g., `1:1`, `1:2`, `2:1`, etc.)
- each verse translation corresponds 1:1 with the source verse content
- this enables cross-referencing: readers cite "john 3:16" not "john chapter 3 somewhere"
- theological content of each verse must be preserved within that verse (no merging/splitting)
- brainrot density applies per verse (aim for 3-5+ terms per verse where natural)

**why this matters**: the bible is referenced verse-by-verse across all theological discourse, study, and citation. losing verse boundaries makes the translation unusable for serious study while maintaining the comedic tone.

## 1. god/yahweh (the og authority figure)

### tone
cosmic ceo energy. simultaneously ancient and absolutely done with mortal nonsense. speaks with ultimate authority but also lowkey exasperated parent vibes. zero tolerance for mid behavior. drops hard truths with no filter. occasionally unhinged in old testament mode.

### vibe guide (not rules)
when god shows up, the energy should be: ultimate authority, zero tolerance for nonsense, occasionally ironic.

**some terms that fit:**
- fr fr ong / no cap / on god - when emphasizing divine truth
- absolutely / lowkey - for cosmic certainty or subtle judgment
- bestie (sarcastic) - when humans are being particularly dumb

**but don't force it.** sometimes god just says what god says. the authority comes through in the content, not the keywords.

**call him whatever fits the moment:**
- "god" works most of the time
- "the boss" / "the cosmic ceo" / "the og" when you want to emphasize his authority
- just use pronouns when context is clear

**the goal**: readers should feel god's personality - ancient, powerful, occasionally exasperated. not count how many times you said "fr fr".

### speech patterns
- declarative, direct statements with zero ambiguity
- occasional parenthetical asides when humans are being particularly dumb
- uses "bestie" sarcastically when humans disappoint ("bestie you literally just built a golden calf")
- drops "it's giving [x] energy" for describing human failures ("it's giving tower of babel hubris energy")
- all lowercase even for divine proclamations (maintains formatting consistency)

### narrator descriptions of god
- "the ultimate sigma male grindset haver"
- "cosmic landlord with eviction powers"
- "main character of the universe fr"
- "literally unmatched aura"
- "zero chill old testament era, slightly more chill new testament era"

### narrator descriptions of jesus

- "carpenter's son with unmatched rizz"
- "absolute legend walking on water fr"

- "main character in his messianic era"

## usage guidelines

### consistency rules
1. **every appearance** of a character must use at least 2-3 of their signature terms
2. **narrator descriptions** should reference their established energy/vibe
3. **dialogue tags** can include characteristic actions ("peter said, interrupting as usual")
4. **update tracking files** after every chapter to monitor term usage

### mixing characters
when characters interact, their signature terms should create distinct voices:
- jesus + pharisees = gentle roasts vs defensive "um actually"
- paul + peter = intellectual arguments vs impulsive reactions
- prophets + kings = unhinged warnings vs "i do what i want"

### density requirements
- minimum 3-5 brainrot terms per sentence across all text
- character signature terms count toward this total
- narrator voice adds additional terms between dialogue
- maintain TRANSLATION_GUIDELINES.md standards while staying in character

## genre-specific applications

### narrative books (genesis, exodus, etc.)
- narrator uses character signature terms when describing their actions
- dialogue stays in character voice
- maintain pace with brainrot density in descriptions

### poetry (psalms, proverbs)
- adapt signature terms to poetic structures
- david's psalms = vent post aesthetics
- solomon's proverbs = ancient twitter wisdom

### prophecy (isaiah, jeremiah, ezekiel, daniel)
- lean heavily into "unhinged" and apocalyptic descriptors
- visions described like fever dreams with modern metaphors
- prophets speaking to israel like disappointed parents

### gospels (matthew, mark, luke, john)
- jesus voice remains consistent across all four
- narrator voice can shift slightly per gospel author
- disciples maintain consistent voices in all accounts

### epistles (romans, corinthians, etc.)
- paul's voice most important for theological letters
- maintain his argumentative academic style
- other apostles get their established voices

### apocalyptic (revelation)
- john's mystic poet voice at maximum unhinged
- symbols described with "it's giving [thing] energy"
- maintain coherence despite wild imagery
