# Book 5, Chapter 4: Philosopher Kings Revealed (The Knowledge vs Opinion Beef)

## The Ultimate Wake-Up Call

"yeah bestie that person is literally dreaming no cap."

"but fr consider the other type, who recognizes absolute beauty exists and can actually tell the difference between the eternal form and all the things that participate in it, who doesn't mix up the objects with the form or the form with the objects—is this person dreaming or absolutely awake?"

"they're wide awake fr fr."

"and can we say the mind of someone who actually knows has real knowledge, while the mind of the other person who just has opinions has, well, opinion?"

"absolutely."

## Coping With The Opinion-Havers

"but bestie what if that second person gets pressed and wants to beef with us about this take, can we give them some copium or advice without exposing that their whole brain is running on dial-up?"

"we definitely gotta offer them some good advice," he replied.

"alright pull up then, let's think of something to say to them. should we start by telling them they're welcome to whatever knowledge they think they have, and we're lowkey happy for them? but we wanna ask them something: does someone who has knowledge know something or nothing? (you answer for them bestie.)"

"i'll say they know something."

"something that exists or doesn't exist?"

"something that exists; cause how can you know something that literally doesn't exist?"

"and are we absolutely certain, after checking this from every angle like we're debugging code, that absolute being can be absolutely known, but the completely non-existent is completely unknowable?"

"nothing could be more certain no cap."

## The Middle Ground Situation

"based. but if there's something that's giving both existing and not-existing energy at the same time, wouldn't that be stuck somewhere between pure being and absolute nothingness?"

"yeah, right between them."

"and since knowledge corresponds to being and ignorance necessarily corresponds to not-being, for that intermediate thing between being and not-being we gotta find a corresponding intermediate between ignorance and knowledge, if such a thing exists?"

"definitely."

"do we admit opinion exists?"

"absolutely."

"is it the same as knowledge, or a different faculty?"

"different faculty fr."

"then opinion and knowledge deal with different types of content corresponding to this difference in faculties?"

"yeah."

## Faculty Check

"and knowledge relates to being and knows being. but wait before i continue let me make a distinction."

"what distinction?"

"i'm gonna put faculties in their own category: they're powers in us and everything else that let us do what we do. sight and hearing, for example, i'd call faculties. you get what category i mean?"

"yeah i totally understand."

"then hear me out on this. i can't see faculties, so the usual distinctions like shape, color, and all that which help me tell different things apart don't apply to them. when talking about a faculty i only think about its sphere and its result; whatever has the same sphere and same result i call the same faculty, but whatever has a different sphere and different result i call different. that how you'd describe it?"

"yeah."

"and bestie would you say knowledge is a faculty, or what category would you put it in?"

"definitely knowledge is a faculty, and lowkey the most op faculty of all."

## Opinion vs Knowledge Arc

"and is opinion also a faculty?"

"absolutely," he said; "cause opinion is what lets us form opinions."

"but wait didn't you just admit a minute ago that knowledge isn't the same as opinion?"

"why would any person with a functioning brain ever say something infallible is the same as something that's constantly taking l's?"

"fire answer, proving," i said, "that we're totally aware there's a difference between them."

"yeah."

"then knowledge and opinion having different powers also have different spheres or subject matters?"

"that's certain."

"being is the sphere or subject matter of knowledge, and knowledge is to know the nature of being?"

"yeah."

"and opinion is to have an opinion?"

"yeah."

"and do we know what we have opinions about? or is the subject matter of opinion the same as the subject matter of knowledge?"

"nah," he replied, "we already debunked that; if difference in faculty means difference in sphere or subject matter, and if, like we said, opinion and knowledge are different faculties, then the sphere of knowledge and opinion can't be the same."

## The Opinion Paradox

"then if being is the subject matter of knowledge, something else must be the subject matter of opinion?"

"yeah, something else."

"well then, is not-being the subject matter of opinion? or wait, how can there even be an opinion about not-being? think about it: when someone has an opinion, don't they have an opinion about something? can they have an opinion that's an opinion about literally nothing?"

"impossible."

"someone who has an opinion has an opinion about some one thing?"

"yeah."

"and not-being isn't one thing but, properly speaking, literally nothing?"

"true."

"of not-being, ignorance was assumed to be the necessary correlative; of being, knowledge?"

"true," he said.

"then opinion isn't concerned with either being or not-being?"

"not with either."

"and therefore can't be either ignorance or knowledge?"

"that seems to be true."

## Finding Opinion's Home

"but is opinion found outside and beyond either of them, clearer than knowledge or darker than ignorance?"

"neither."

"then i guess opinion seems to you darker than knowledge but lighter than ignorance?"

"both; and by a lot fr."

"and also between them?"

"yeah."

"then you'd say opinion is intermediate?"

"no question."

"but weren't we saying earlier that if anything appeared to be the type that both is and isn't at the same time, that type of thing would also be in the interval between pure being and absolute not-being; and the corresponding faculty is neither knowledge nor ignorance, but will be found in the interval between them?"

"true."

"and in that interval we've now discovered something we call opinion?"

"we have."

## The Ultimate Call-Out

"then what's left to discover is the object that participates equally in the nature of being and not-being, and can't properly be called either pure and simple; this unknown thing, when discovered, we can truly call the subject of opinion, and assign each to their proper faculty—the extremes to the faculties of the extremes and the mean to the faculty of the mean."

"true."

"with all that established, i wanna ask the person who thinks there's no absolute or unchangeable form of beauty—whose whole vibe is that beauty is just the many beautiful things—this person, your average beauty stan who absolutely cannot handle being told that the beautiful is one, and the just is one, or that anything is one—to them i'd say, bestie would you be so kind as to tell us whether, of all these beautiful things, there's one that won't sometimes look ugly; or of the just things, that won't sometimes seem unjust; or of the holy things, that won't also be unholy?"

"no," he replied; "the beautiful will from some angle look ugly; and same goes for everything else."

"and can't the many doubles also be halves?—doubles of one thing and halves of another?"

"absolutely true."

"and things called great and small, heavy and light, won't they be described by these names just as much as by the opposite names?"

"true; both these and the opposite names will always apply to all of them."

## The Riddle Energy

"and can any one of those many things that get called by specific names be said to be this rather than not be this?"

he replied: "they're giving those riddles people tell at parties or that children's puzzle about the eunuch aiming at the bat, with what he hit it with, as they say in the puzzle, and what the bat was sitting on. the individual objects i'm talking about are also a riddle and have double meanings: you can't lock them in your mind as either being or not-being, or both, or neither."

"then what you gonna do with them?" i said. "can they have a better spot than between being and not-being? cause they're clearly not in greater darkness or negation than not-being, or more full of light and existence than being."

"that's absolutely true," he said.

## The Final Distinction

"so then we seem to have discovered that the many ideas the masses have about beauty and everything else are just vibing in some region that's halfway between pure being and pure not-being?"

"we have."

"yeah; and we agreed earlier that anything like this we might find should be described as matter of opinion, not matter of knowledge; being the intermediate flux that gets caught and detained by the intermediate faculty."

"absolutely true."

"then those who see the many beautiful things, but never see absolute beauty and can't follow any guide who tries to show them the way; who see the many just things but not absolute justice, and so on—these people can be said to have opinion but not knowledge?"

"that's certain."

"but those who see the absolute and eternal and immutable can be said to know, and not just have opinion?"

"can't deny that either."

"one group loves and embraces the subjects of knowledge, the other those of opinion? the latter are the same ones, remember bestie, who vibe to sweet sounds and look at pretty colors but absolutely refuse to admit absolute beauty exists."

"yeah i remember."

## Philosophers vs Opinion-Lovers

"so would we be out of pocket calling them lovers of opinion rather than lovers of wisdom, and will they get big mad at us for describing them like that?"

"i'll tell them not to be pressed; nobody should be mad at the truth."

"but those who love the truth in each thing should be called lovers of wisdom and not lovers of opinion."

"absolutely fr fr ong."
