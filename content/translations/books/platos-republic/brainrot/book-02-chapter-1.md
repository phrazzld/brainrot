# Book 2, Chapter 1: Glaucon Goes Full Devil's Advocate & The Ring of Gyges Goes Viral

## Glaucon Wants the Real Tea, Not the Fake News

so i thought i was literally done with this whole justice debate but turns out that was just the tutorial level. glaucon, who's always ready to throw hands intellectually, was absolutely not satisfied with thrasymachus rage-quitting the argument. he pulled up on me like: "socrates, do you actually want to convince us that being just is always better than being unjust, or are you just trying to SEEM like you convinced us?"

"i'd genuinely want to convince you fr fr if i could," i replied.

"well you absolutely haven't then. let me ask you something - aren't there different categories of good things? like some things we want just because they slap on their own, not for what they lead to? like harmless pleasures and vibes that just hit different in the moment even if nothing comes from them?"

"yeah there's definitely that category," i agreed.

"and isn't there a second type of good things like knowledge, sight, and health that are fire both for themselves AND their results?"

"facts," i said.

"and wouldn't you say there's a third category like working out, being sick and getting treatment, or grinding for money - these are mid experiences but we do them for the benefits they bring?"

"yeah that third category exists too. but why you asking?"

"because i need to know which category you're putting justice in."

"the goated tier," i replied, "with things that people who want to be genuinely happy desire both for their own sake and for what comes from them."

"well most people think that's cap. they think justice belongs in the annoying category - things you only do for clout and rewards but that actually suck to deal with."

"i know that's what they think," i said, "and that's literally what thrasymachus was preaching when he was flaming justice and gassing up injustice. but i'm too much of a smoothbrain to be convinced by him."

## The Three-Part Argument Gets Announced

"i want you to hear me out too, not just him," glaucon said, "then we'll see if we're on the same wavelength. because thrasymachus seems like he got hypnotized by your philosophical rizz way too early. but to me, nobody's properly explained what justice and injustice actually ARE yet. forget about their rewards and consequences - i want to know what they do to your soul when nobody's watching."

"so if you're cool with it, i'm gonna resurrect thrasymachus's argument. first, i'll explain what people think justice is and where it comes from. second, i'll show that everyone who practices justice is basically forced to and hates it. third, i'll argue they're being rational because the unjust life absolutely mogs the just life - at least according to what people say."

"and just to be clear socrates, i don't actually believe this myself. but i'm so confused hearing thrasymachus and literally thousands of other people preaching this, while i've never heard anyone properly defend justice for its own sake. i want to hear justice get hyped up just for being justice, and you're literally the best person to do it."

"so i'm about to go absolutely ham defending the unjust life, and the way i argue will show you how i want you to defend justice and roast injustice. you good with this plan?"

"bro i literally can't think of a better topic for people with brains to discuss."

"bet," he replied, "so let me start with the nature and origin of justice like i said."

## The Social Contract Theory (But Make It Cynical)

"people say that doing injustice is naturally poggers while suffering injustice is cringe. but the cringe of being a victim is worse than the pog of being the perpetrator. so when people have been on both sides - griefing others and getting griefed - and can't consistently avoid one and get the other, they decide to make a deal: nobody griefs anybody."

"that's where laws and social contracts come from. whatever the law says becomes 'lawful' and 'just.' so justice is literally just a compromise between the best case scenario (griefing others with zero consequences) and the worst case (getting griefed with no way to clap back). justice sits in the middle - people don't love it because it's good, they tolerate it because they're too weak to get away with injustice."

"anyone who actually had the power to be unjust and get away with it would never agree to this deal - they'd have to be absolutely cooked to accept that. anyway socrates, that's the mainstream theory about what justice is and where it comes from."

## The Ring of Gyges Story (Ancient Invisible Mode Exploit)

"now to prove that people only act justly because they're forced to, not because they want to, imagine this scenario: we give both a just person and an unjust person the power to do literally whatever they want. then we follow them and see where their desires lead. we'd catch the just person red-handed going down the exact same path as the unjust person, chasing what every person naturally wants - their own benefit. they only act 'just' because the law forces them to."

"the perfect example of this power would be something like what happened to gyges's ancestor. according to the lore, this dude was a shepherd working for the king of lydia. there was a massive storm and earthquake that opened up a crack in the ground where he was watching his sheep. man was absolutely shook but curious, so he went down into the hole."

"down there among other wild stuff, he found this huge bronze horse that was hollow with doors on it. he looked inside and saw a dead body that seemed bigger than a normal human, wearing nothing but a gold ring. he yoinked the ring and climbed back out."

"later at the monthly shepherd meeting where they reported to the king, he showed up wearing the ring. while sitting there, he accidentally turned the ring's setting inward on his hand and instantly became invisible. everyone started talking about him like he'd left. he was mind-blown and when he turned the ring back outward, he reappeared. after testing it multiple times with the same result - turn it in for invisible mode, turn it out to be visible - he immediately got himself chosen as a messenger to the royal court."

"as soon as he got there, he seduced the queen, and together they murdered the king and took over the kingdom. absolute power move."

## Everyone Would Use the Invisibility Exploit

"now imagine if there were two rings like this and we gave one to a just person and one to an unjust person. literally nobody has that much self-control to stay just. nobody would keep their hands to themselves when they could steal anything from the market, break into any house, sleep with anyone, kill or free anyone from prison - basically be a god among NPCs."

"the just person's actions would be identical to the unjust person's. they'd both end up in the same place. this is literally proof that nobody is just voluntarily or because they think it's good for them personally. they're only just out of necessity - wherever someone thinks they can safely be unjust, they absolutely will be."

"everyone believes deep down that injustice is way more profitable personally than justice, and according to this argument, they're right. if someone got this invisibility power and never did anything wrong or touched other people's stuff, everyone watching would think they're the biggest loser ever (even though they'd praise them to their faces because they're scared of getting griefed themselves). facts."

## The Ultimate Thought Experiment

"now if we really want to judge whether the just or unjust life is better, we need to separate them completely. no half-measures. how do we do this? like this: let the unjust person be maximum unjust and the just person maximum just. don't nerf either of them - let them both be perfect at their respective lifestyles."

"first, let the unjust person be like any world-class expert. like an elite pilot or doctor who knows exactly what they can and can't do and stays within their limits, recovering quickly from any Ls. let the unjust person pull off their schemes perfectly and stay undetected if they want to be truly goated at injustice (getting caught is literally skill issue behavior)."

"the peak of injustice is seeming just when you're actually not. so our perfectly unjust person needs perfect injustice - no deductions. let them do the most heinous stuff while maintaining a reputation for being an absolute saint. if they slip up, they need to be able to recover. they need god-tier persuasion skills if anything comes to light, plus the strength and connections to force their way through when needed."

## The Just Person Gets the Worst Possible Setup

"now next to them, let's place the just person - simple and noble, someone who actually wants to BE good, not just SEEM good (as aeschylus said). we have to remove any appearance of being just, because if they seem just, they'll get honor and rewards. then we won't know if they're being just for justice itself or for the clout and benefits."

"so strip them of everything except justice itself. make their situation the complete opposite of the unjust person. despite being the best person ever, let them have the absolute worst reputation. let them be considered trash tier until death, staying just while seeming unjust. when both have reached their final forms - one peak justice, one peak injustice - then we can judge who's actually happier."

"damn glaucon!" i said. "you're really min-maxing these character builds for comparison like you're polishing statues for a competition."

"i'm doing my best," he said. "and now that we know what they're like, it's easy to describe what kind of life each one gets. let me paint the picture - but if it sounds too brutal, remember these aren't my words socrates, they're from people who stan injustice over justice."

## The Just Person's Speedrun to Suffering

"they'll say the just person with this setup will get whipped, tortured, chained up, have their eyes burned out, and finally after suffering literally every possible L, they'll be crucified. then they'll learn they should've focused on SEEMING just, not BEING just."

"those aeschylus lines actually fit the unjust person better than the just one. the unjust person is chasing actual results, not living for appearances - they want to BE unjust, not just seem unjust:"

"'their mind has deep and fertile soil from which their gigabrain schemes grow'"

"first, they seem just so they get political power. they can marry whoever they want, do business deals with whoever, always getting the better end because they dgaf about being unfair. they win every competition, public or private, getting rich off everyone else's Ls. with that money they can help their friends and grief their enemies."

"they can make huge fancy sacrifices to the gods, honor gods and humans way better than just people can. so logically the gods probably like them more than just people. and that's how gods and humans supposedly team up to make the unjust person's life better than the just person's, socrates."

## Adeimantus Joins the Combo Attack

i was about to respond to glaucon when his brother adeimantus jumped in: "socrates, you don't think that's all the arguments, do you?"

"what else is there?" i asked.

"the strongest point hasn't even been mentioned," he replied.

"well, as they say, 'brother's got brother's back' - if glaucon missed anything, you cover it. though honestly what he already said has me completely body-bagged and unable to defend justice."

"that's cap," he replied. "but let me add something: there's another angle about how people talk about justice and injustice that we need to hear to really understand glaucon's point."

## Parents and Teachers Are Hypocrites

"parents and teachers are always telling kids to be just, but why? not for justice itself, but for the reputation and clout it brings. they want the kid who SEEMS just to get all those marriages, jobs, and benefits glaucon listed that come from having a good reputation."

"but these people take the appearance game even further - they bring in the gods, claiming heaven literally showers blessings on good people. like hesiod says the gods make just people's trees produce food at the top and bees in the middle, their sheep are thicc with wool, and tons of other buffs."

"homer has similar bars about some king whose fame is godlike because they maintain justice, so the earth gives them mad crops, their trees are loaded, sheep always breeding, and the sea provides fish."

"but the rewards for being just get even more insane with musaeus and his crew. they take just people to the afterlife and have them eternally turnt at a feast, wearing flower crowns and getting infinitely drunk - like the ultimate reward for virtue is being permanently wasted. some go even further saying the just person's family line gets blessed for generations."

"meanwhile for bad people, they get buried in underworld mud and have to carry water in a broken bucket forever. even while alive they get cancelled and suffer all the punishments glaucon said just people get when they have bad reputations. that's literally all they've got for praising justice and roasting injustice."

## The Prosperity Gospel Hits Different

"but socrates, there's another way people talk about justice and injustice that's not just from poets. literally everyone says justice and virtue are based but absolutely exhausting and difficult, while vice and injustice are easy mode and only considered bad because of laws and social pressure."

"they say being dishonest is usually more profitable than being honest. they're totally ready to call rich bad people successful and respect them publicly and privately, while completely ignoring poor good people even though they admit the poor ones are actually better."

"but the wildest part is how they talk about virtue and the gods. they say gods give good people terrible lives full of suffering while bad people get blessed. then you got these grifter prophets going to rich people's doors claiming they have god-given powers to fix any sins you or your ancestors did through sacrifices and rituals with parties and feasts."

"they also claim they can hex your enemies (whether they deserve it or not) for a small fee, saying they've got magic that makes the gods do what they want. and they quote poets as proof, like hesiod saying:"

"'vice is everywhere and easy to get, the path is smooth and it's right there. but the gods put grinding in front of virtue'"

"and they quote homer about influencing gods, saying the gods can be swayed by sacrifices and prayers when people have messed up. they've got whole libraries supposedly by musaeus and orpheus (who they claim were children of the moon and muses) that they use for their rituals."

"they convince not just individuals but entire cities that you can get absolution for sins through sacrifices and 'mystery rites' that work for both living and dead people. they say these 'mysteries' save us from hell, but if you don't participate, terrible things await you."

## Young People Are Getting the Wrong Message

"so when young smart people hear all this, flitting from idea to idea like bees collecting nectar, what conclusions do they draw about how to live their best life? they probably quote pindar to themselves:"

"'should i climb to success through justice or through elaborate deception?'"

"because what they're hearing is: if i'm actually just but don't seem just, i get nothing but pain and losses. but if i'm unjust but seem just, i get a godlike life. and since 'appearance dominates reality' as the wise ones say, and appearance controls happiness, that's where i should focus all my energy."

"i'll build a whole facade of virtue around myself as my public image, but behind it i'll be the cunning fox that archilochus (the goat himself) recommended. 'but it's hard to keep being evil secret!' someone might say. well duh, nothing great is easy."

## The Villain's Optimization Guide

"but the argument shows this is the path to happiness. to stay hidden we'll form secret societies and political clubs. there are rhetoric teachers who'll teach us how to manipulate courts and assemblies. so between persuasion and force, we'll get illegal gains without punishment."

"'but you can't hide from or overpower the gods!' okay but what if gods don't exist? or what if they don't care about humans? then why should we care about hiding? and if gods do exist and care, we only know about them from stories and poets - the same sources that say gods can be bribed with sacrifices and prayers."

"we need to be consistent - either believe both claims or neither. if the poets are right, we should be unjust and use our ill-gotten gains to bribe the gods. being just means we won't get divine punishment but we miss out on the benefits of injustice. being unjust gets us the gains, and then we can pray away the consequences."

"'but there's punishment in the afterlife for our crimes!' sure bestie, but that's what mystery rites and absolution gods are for - that's what major cities and children of gods (poets and prophets) tell us."

## The Final Boss Question

"so given all this socrates, why would anyone with any intelligence, strength, status, or wealth choose justice over injustice? why wouldn't they literally laugh when they hear justice being praised? even if someone could prove everything i've said is cap and knows justice is best, they're not mad at unjust people - they're understanding."

"they know people are only just because they're forced to be, unless maybe some divine nature makes them hate injustice or they've gained knowledge of the truth. but everyone else? they only criticize injustice because they're too weak, old, or cowardly to do it themselves. proof: as soon as they get power, they immediately become as unjust as possible."

i told them what i honestly thought: "this investigation is going to be extremely difficult and needs sharp eyes. since we're not exactly built different intellectually, i think we should use this method: imagine someone with mid eyesight was asked to read tiny text from far away, then someone realized the same text exists somewhere else but way bigger - they could read the big version first then check if the small one matches. absolute genius move right?"

## The Origin of Society (Economics 101)

"a state exists," i said, "because none of us is self-sufficient. we all need mad stuff. can you think of any other reason states form?"

"nah, that's it."

"so since we have tons of needs and need lots of people to meet them, we gather helpers and partners for different things. when all these partners and helpers live together, we call that a state."

"true," he said.

"and they trade with each other, giving and receiving, because they think it benefits them."

"facts."

"so let's create a state from scratch in our minds. the true creator is necessity - the mother of invention."

"for sure," he replied.

"the first and most important necessity is food to stay alive."

"absolutely."

"second is housing, third is clothing and stuff like that."

"right."

"so how will our city provide all this? we'll need a farmer, a builder, a weaver - should we add a shoemaker and other people who take care of physical needs?"

"sounds good."

"the most basic state needs at least four or five people."

"clearly."

"so how should they work? should each person make everything for everyone? like should the farmer produce food for four people, spending four times the effort to share with others? or should they only make food for themselves and spend the other three-quarters of their time making their own house, clothes, and shoes, not sharing with others but being self-sufficient?"

adeimantus said: "the first way is probably easier, socrates - just making food and trading."

"you know what, that makes sense," i replied. "and now that you mention it, i realize we're not all the same - different people are naturally good at different things."

"very true."
