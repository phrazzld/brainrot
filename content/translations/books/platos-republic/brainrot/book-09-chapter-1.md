# Book 9, Chapter 1: The Tyrannical Man - The Ultimate Character Study Arc

## The Final Boss Analysis

"last of all comes the tyrannical person; about whom we have once more to ask, how are they formed out of the democratical? and how do they live, in happiness or in misery?"

"yes," he said, "they're the only one remaining."

"there is, however," i said, "a previous question which remains unanswered."

"what question?"

"i don't think that we've adequately determined the nature and number of the appetites, and until this is accomplished the inquiry will always be confused."

"well," he said, "it's not too late to supply the omission."

## The Appetite Classification System (Absolutely Fire)

"very true," i said; "and observe the point which i want to understand: certain of the unnecessary pleasures and appetites i conceive to be unlawful; everyone appears to have them, but in some persons they're controlled by the laws and by reason, and the better desires prevail over them—either they're wholly banished or they become few and weak; while in the case of others they're stronger, and there are more of them."

"which appetites do you mean?"

## The Wild Beast Within Theory (Peak Psychology)

"i mean those which are awake when the reasoning and human and ruling power is asleep; then the wild beast within us, gorged with meat or drink, starts up and having shaken off sleep, goes forth to satisfy their desires; and there's no conceivable folly or crime—not excepting incest or any other unnatural union, or parricide, or the eating of forbidden food—which at such a time, when they've parted company with all shame and sense, a person may not be ready to commit."

"most true," he said.

## The Based Sleep Hygiene Protocol

"but when a person's pulse is healthy and temperate, and when before going to sleep they've awakened their rational powers, and fed them on noble thoughts and inquiries, collecting themselves in meditation; after having first indulged their appetites neither too much nor too little, but just enough to lay them to sleep, and prevent them and their enjoyments and pains from interfering with the higher principle—which they leave in the solitude of pure abstraction, free to contemplate and aspire to the knowledge of the unknown, whether in past, present, or future: when again they've allayed the passionate element, if they have a quarrel against anyone—i say, when, after pacifying the two irrational principles, they rouse up the third, which is reason, before they take their rest, then, as you know, they attain truth most nearly, and are least likely to be the sport of fantastic and lawless visions."

"i quite agree."

## The Universal Dark Side

"in saying this i've been running into a digression; but the point which i desire to note is that in all of us, even in good people, there's a lawless wild-beast nature, which peers out in sleep. pray, consider whether i'm right, and you agree with me."

"yes, i agree."

## The Democratic Person's Origin Recap

"and now remember the character which we attributed to the democratic person. they were supposed from their youth upwards to have been trained under a miserly parent, who encouraged the saving appetites in them, but discountenanced the unnecessary, which aim only at amusement and ornament?"

"true."

"and then they got into the company of a more refined, licentious sort of people, and taking to all their wanton ways rushed into the opposite extreme from an abhorrence of their father's meanness. at last, being a better person than their corruptors, they were drawn in both directions until they halted midway and led a life, not of vulgar and slavish passion, but of what they deemed moderate indulgence in various pleasures. after this manner the democrat was generated out of the oligarch?"

"yes," he said; "that was our view of them, and is so still."

## The Next Generation Problem

"and now," i said, "years will have passed away, and you must conceive this person, such as they are, to have a child, who's brought up in their parent's principles."

"i can imagine them."

"then you must further imagine the same thing to happen to the child which has already happened to the parent:—they're drawn into a perfectly lawless life, which by their seducers is termed perfect liberty; and their parent and friends take part with their moderate desires, and the opposite party assist the opposite ones. as soon as these dire magicians and tyrant-makers find that they're losing their hold on them, they contrive to implant in them a master passion, to be lord over their idle and spendthrift lusts—a sort of monstrous winged drone—that's the only image which will adequately describe them."

"yes," he said, "that's the only adequate image of them."

## The Winged Drone Takeover (Absolutely Cinema)

"and when their other lusts, amid clouds of incense and perfumes and garlands and wines, and all the pleasures of a dissolute life, now let loose, come buzzing around them, nourishing to the utmost the sting of desire which they implant in their drone-like nature, then at last this lord of the soul, having madness for the captain of their guard, breaks out into a frenzy: and if they find in themselves any good opinions or appetites in process of formation, and there's in them any sense of shame remaining, to these better principles they put an end, and cast them forth until they've purged away temperance and brought in madness to the full."

"yes," he said, "that's the way in which the tyrannical person is generated."

## The Tyrant Nature Revealed

"and isn't this the reason why of old love has been called a tyrant?"

"i shouldn't wonder."

"further," i said, "hasn't a drunk person also the spirit of a tyrant?"

"they have."

"and you know that a person who's deranged and not right in their mind, will fancy that they're able to rule, not only over people, but also over the gods?"

"that they will."

"and the tyrannical person in the true sense of the word comes into being when, either under the influence of nature, or habit, or both, they become drunk, lustful, passionate? o bestie, isn't that so?"

"assuredly."

## The Tyrannical Lifestyle (Peak Chaos)

"such is the person and such is their origin. and next, how do they live?"

"suppose, as people facetiously say, you were to tell me."

"i imagine," i said, "at the next step in their progress, that there'll be feasts and carousals and revellings and courtesans, and all that sort of thing; love is the lord of the house within them, and orders all the concerns of their soul."

"that's certain."

"yes; and every day and every night desires grow up many and formidable, and their demands are many."

"they are indeed," he said.

## The Financial Spiral

"their revenues, if they have any, are soon spent."

"true."

"then comes debt and the cutting down of their property."

"of course."

"when they have nothing left, must not their desires, crowding in the nest like young ravens, be crying aloud for food; and they, goaded on by them, and especially by love themselves, who's in a manner the captain of them, is in a frenzy, and would fain discover whom they can defraud or despoil of their property, in order that they may gratify them?"

"yes, that's sure to be the case."

"they must have money, no matter how, if they're to escape horrid pains and pangs."

"they must."

## The Family Crime Arc

"and as in themselves there was a succession of pleasures, and the new got the better of the old and took away their rights, so they being younger will claim to have more than their parent and their mother, and if they've spent their own share of the property, they'll take a slice of theirs."

"no doubt they will."

"and if their parents won't give way, then they'll try first of all to cheat and deceive them."

"very true."

"and if they fail, then they'll use force and plunder them."

"yes, probably."

"and if the old man and woman fight for their own, what then, bestie? will the creature feel any compunction at tyrannizing over them?"

"nay," he said, "i shouldn't feel at all comfortable about their parents."

## The Ultimate Family Betrayal (Actually Unhinged)

"but, o heavens! adeimantus, on account of some new-fangled love of a harlot, who's anything but a necessary connection, can you believe that they would strike the mother who's their ancient friend and necessary to their very existence, and would place her under the authority of the other, when she's brought under the same roof with her; or that, under like circumstances, they would do the same to their withered old father, first and most indispensable of friends, for the sake of some newly-found blooming youth who's the reverse of indispensable?"

"yes, indeed," he said; "i believe that they would."

"truly, then," i said, "a tyrannical child is a blessing to their father and mother."

"they are indeed," he replied.

## The Crime Spree Progression

"they first take their property, and when that fails, and pleasures are beginning to swarm in the hive of their soul, then they break into a house, or steal the garments of some nightly wayfarer; next they proceed to clear a temple. meanwhile the old opinions which they had when a child, and which gave judgment about good and evil, are overthrown by those others which have just been emancipated, and are now the body-guard of love and share their empire. these in their democratic days, when they were still subject to the laws and to their parent, were only let loose in the dreams of sleep. but now that they're under the dominion of love, they become always and in waking reality what they were then very rarely and in a dream only; they'll commit the foulest murder, or eat forbidden food, or be guilty of any other horrid act. love is their tyrant, and lives lordly in them and lawlessly, and being themselves a king, leads them on, as a tyrant leads a state, to the performance of any reckless deed by which they can maintain themselves and the rabble of their associates, whether those whom evil communications have brought in from without, or those whom they themselves have allowed to break loose within them by reason of a similar evil nature in themselves. have we not here a picture of their way of life?"

"yes, indeed," he said.

## The Criminal Network

"and if there are only a few of them in the state, and the rest of the people are well disposed, they go away and become the body-guard or mercenary soldiers of some other tyrant who may probably want them for a war; and if there's no war, they stay at home and do many little pieces of mischief in the city."

"what sort of mischief?"

"for example, they're the thieves, burglars, pickpockets, foot-pads, robbers of temples, kidnappers of the community; or if they're able to speak they turn informers, and bear false witness, and take bribes."

"a small catalogue of evils, even if the perpetrators of them are few in number."

## The Tyrant Selection Process

"yes," i said; "but small and great are comparative terms, and all these things, in the misery and evil which they inflict upon a state, don't come within a thousand miles of the tyrant; when this noxious class and their followers grow numerous and become conscious of their strength, assisted by the infatuation of the people, they choose from among themselves the one who has most of the tyrant in their own soul, and them they create their tyrant."

"yes," he said, "and they'll be the most fit to be a tyrant."

## The Ultimate Parricide (State Edition)

"if the people yield, well and good; but if they resist them, as they began by beating their own father and mother, so now, if they have the power, they beat them, and will keep their dear old fatherland or motherland, as the cretans say, in subjection to their young retainers whom they've introduced to be their rulers and masters. this is the end of their passions and desires."

"exactly."

## The Tyrannical Social Pattern

"when such people are only private individuals and before they get power, this is their character; they associate entirely with their own flatterers or ready tools; or if they want anything from anybody, they in their turn are equally ready to bow down before them: they profess every sort of affection for them; but when they've gained their point they know them no more."

"yes, truly."

"they're always either the masters or servants and never the friends of anybody; the tyrant never tastes of true freedom or friendship."

"certainly not."

"and may we not rightly call such people treacherous?"

"no question."

"also they're utterly unjust, if we were right in our notion of justice?"

"yes," he said, "and we were perfectly right."

## The Ultimate Character Summary

"let's then sum up in a word," i said, "the character of the worst person: they're the waking reality of what we dreamed."

"most true."

"and this is they who being by nature most of a tyrant bears rule, and the longer they live the more of a tyrant they become."

"that's certain," said glaucon, taking his turn to answer.

## The Misery Question

"and won't they who have been shown to be the wickedest, be also the most miserable? and they who have tyrannized longest and most, most continually and truly miserable; although this may not be the opinion of people in general?"

"yes," he said, "inevitably."

"and must not the tyrannical person be like the tyrannical state, and the democratical person like the democratical state; and the same of the others?"

"certainly."

"and as state is to state in virtue and happiness, so is person in relation to person?"

"to be sure."

## The State Comparison

"then comparing our original city, which was under a king, and the city which is under a tyrant, how do they stand as to virtue?"

"they're the opposite extremes," he said, "cause one is the very best and the other is the very worst."

"there can be no mistake," i said, "as to which is which, and therefore i'll at once inquire whether you would arrive at a similar decision about their relative happiness and misery. and here we must not allow ourselves to be panic-stricken at the apparition of the tyrant, who's only a unit and may perhaps have a few retainers about them; but let's go as we ought into every corner of the city and look all about, and then we'll give our opinion."

"a fair invitation," he replied; "and i see, as everyone must, that a tyranny is the wretchedest form of government, and the rule of a king the happiest."

## The Judging Criteria

"and in estimating the people too, may i not fairly make a like request, that i should have a judge whose mind can enter into and see through human nature? they must not be like a child who looks at the outside and is dazzled at the pompous aspect which the tyrannical nature assumes to the beholder, but let them be one who has a clear insight. may i suppose that the judgment is given in the hearing of us all by one who's able to judge, and has dwelt in the same place with them, and been present at their daily life and known them in their family relations, where they may be seen stripped of their tragedy attire, and again in the hour of public danger—they shall tell us about the happiness and misery of the tyrant when compared with other people?"

"that again," he said, "is a very fair proposal."

"shall i assume that we ourselves are able and experienced judges and have before now met with such a person? we shall then have someone who'll answer our inquiries."

"by all means."

## The Soul-State Parallel

"let me ask you not to forget the parallel of the individual and the state; bearing this in mind, and glancing in turn from one to the other of them, will you tell me their respective conditions?"

"what do you mean?" he asked.

"beginning with the state," i replied, "would you say that a city which is governed by a tyrant is free or enslaved?"

"no city," he said, "can be more completely enslaved."

"and yet, as you see, there are freemen as well as masters in such a state?"

"yes," he said, "i see that there are—a few; but the people, speaking generally, and the best of them are miserably degraded and enslaved."

"then if the person is like the state," i said, "must not the same rule prevail? their soul is full of meanness and vulgarity—the best elements in them are enslaved; and there's a small ruling part, which is also the worst and maddest."
